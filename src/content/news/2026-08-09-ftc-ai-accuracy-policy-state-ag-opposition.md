summary: >-
  The FTC's proposed policy statement says AI companies imply their systems
  are neutral, so an undisclosed safety adjustment to outputs could count as
  consumer deception under Section 5. In formal comments filed August 3, EFF,
  Public Knowledge, Fight for the Future and the Center for Democracy and
  Technology argued no neutral baseline exists — the reinforcement learning
  every major developer uses to teach a model to refuse harmful requests is
  not a layer over a neutral model, it is part of what produces the model. A
  separate coalition of attorneys general from California, Colorado and
  roughly 20 other states filed comments the same day.
implications: >-
  The FTC's proposal carves out blocking illegal content, but not the
  broader safety tuning built into most commercial AI products, and doesn't
  define how prominent disclosure must be to avoid liability. State AGs say
  the policy would force AI vendors to choose between state laws requiring
  tuned outputs and a federal deception theory that penalizes tuning
  without disclosure. Any business relying on off-the-shelf AI tools with
  built-in safety filters is downstream of how that resolves.
