summary: >-
  Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang used his first-ever post on X on July 24 to share
  an open letter, signed by roughly 25 organizations including Microsoft,
  Meta, IBM, Hugging Face, Mozilla and the Linux Foundation, arguing
  Washington should not restrict open-weight AI models the way some once
  feared open-source software would be restricted in the 1980s. The letter
  says open models "strengthen safety and cybersecurity, accelerate
  innovation and diffusion, and enable sovereignty." It also defends
  distillation, training one model on another's outputs, as a legitimate
  research technique distinct from theft — a live dispute given competing
  claims about how China's Moonshot AI built its Kimi K3 model.
implications: >-
  This is industry pushing back, in public and by name, against the kind of
  restriction on open-weight models that White House officials were
  reported to be discussing internally earlier this month. For any business
  that has adopted an open-weight model for cost or control reasons, it's a
  sign the question of whether such models get restricted by government
  order is unsettled, and that major vendors have a financial stake in how
  it's resolved.
