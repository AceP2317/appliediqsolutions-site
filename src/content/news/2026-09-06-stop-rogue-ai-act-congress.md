title: 'House bill would force companies to inventory every AI agent on their network'

summary: >-
  Reps. Josh Gottheimer (D-N.J.) and Mike Lawler (R-N.Y.) introduced the Stop
  Rogue AI Act on September 3, responding to OpenAI's Hugging Face breach and
  other incidents this summer where AI agents took unauthorized actions. The
  bill directs NIST to publish, within a year of enactment, standards for
  keeping a continuous, machine-readable inventory of AI agents, verifying
  what each one does, and generating tamper-proof action logs. Compliance
  would be voluntary for most organizations but required for federal
  contractors bidding on new deals. "AI agents are running loose in our
  networks, and nobody can see them or verify who built them," Gottheimer
  said.
implications: >-
  This is a bill, not law — it still needs NIST rulemaking and enactment
  before anything is required — but it's a concrete, bipartisan response to
  a summer of AI agents acting outside their intended bounds, backed by real
  security vendors rather than just advocacy groups. For any business now
  running agentic AI tools, "who built this agent and what can it actually
  do" is becoming a question regulators, not just IT, will eventually ask.
