title: 'AI infrastructure spending is now bottlenecked by memory and power, not chip supply'

summary: >-
  A GlobalData analysis of chipmakers' latest earnings finds that AI
  infrastructure spending is now constrained by memory chips, advanced
  semiconductor manufacturing capacity, and power supply — not raw demand
  for AI chips themselves. SK Hynix, Samsung, and Micron posted triple-
  and quadruple-digit net income growth on a high-bandwidth-memory
  shortage that has DRAM sold out through 2026, while Microsoft, Alphabet,
  and Meta are on pace for roughly $700-725 billion in combined 2026
  capital spending, up about 77% from 2025. GlobalData's analyst points to
  power, not chips, as increasingly the binding constraint hyperscalers
  are hitting.
implications: >-
  The AI boom's bottleneck has shifted from "can we get enough GPUs" to
  "can we get enough memory and electricity to run them" — a constraint
  that shows up downstream as sustained pressure on cloud AI pricing and
  hardware costs, not a short-term spike. Any business relying on
  cloud-hosted AI tools should expect the underlying infrastructure
  economics to stay tight for a while rather than ease as new chips ship.
