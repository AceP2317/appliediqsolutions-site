title: "EU's 'Digital Omnibus' pushes back most of the AI Act's hardest deadlines"

tier: 'standard'
spotlight: false
summary: >-
  The EU's Digital Omnibus on AI — cleared by the Council on June 29, signed
  July 8, and now published — delays the AI Act's high-risk-system
  obligations that were due August 2, 2026: standalone high-risk systems
  (hiring tools, credit scoring, and similar use-based systems) now have
  until December 2, 2027, and AI embedded in regulated products like medical
  devices or machinery until August 2, 2028. It also pushes the requirement
  to machine-mark AI-generated content as such from August 2026 to December
  2026, and gives EU member states an extra year to stand up their AI
  regulatory sandboxes.
implications: >-
  Any business that had budgeted for an August 2 compliance sprint on
  high-risk AI Act obligations now has considerably more runway — in some
  cases over two years. It also means one specific piece of the
  transparency rules covered here last week, the requirement to
  machine-mark AI-generated content, has itself been pushed to December —
  worth re-checking which August deadlines still actually apply to you
  rather than assuming last week's summary is now the complete picture.
