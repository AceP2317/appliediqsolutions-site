spotlight: true
summary: >-
  A Teradata survey of 1,000 senior technology and data leaders found that
  while 90% expect to increase agentic AI investment over the next year,
  only 37% report measurable business impact so far, and 63% say returns
  have been no more than small or emerging. The report, "Arrested
  Automation: Why Agentic AI Stalls at the Enterprise Level," sorts
  companies into a four-stage maturity index — most remain stuck in early
  "experimenting" or "developing" stages, and just 7% have reached the stage
  where AI reliably runs multi-step workflows alone. It attributes the gap
  to firms building enterprise AI on data foundations designed for
  individual productivity, not connected business processes.
implications: >-
  The pattern echoes what the enterprise AI hype cycle keeps producing
  elsewhere: heavy spending commitment well ahead of proven return, with the
  gap explained by messy internal data and process foundations rather than
  model quality. For any business sizing up an AI investment, it's a
  reminder that the bottleneck is rarely the AI itself.
angle: >-
  It's also a case for scope discipline: a narrow tool built around one
  business's own numbers and its own workflow sidesteps the exact failure
  mode this survey describes — bolting broad "agentic" ambitions onto data
  and processes that were never built to support them.
