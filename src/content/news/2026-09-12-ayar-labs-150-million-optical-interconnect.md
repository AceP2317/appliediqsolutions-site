summary: >-
  Ayar Labs said September 10 it extended its 2026 Series E round with an
  additional $150 million from an unnamed investor, bringing the round to
  $650 million and the startup's total outside funding past $1 billion. The
  company makes optical interconnect technology, including a co-packaged
  optics product called TeraPHY, meant to replace the copper links between
  AI chips that CEO Mark Wade says are becoming "the limiting factor for AI
  scale-up" — copper's distance limits, power draw and heat make it harder
  to wire together the larger GPU clusters newer AI systems need. Backers
  include Nvidia, AMD and MediaTek; Wade told Reuters the company aims to
  reach mass production by early 2028.
implications: >-
  The bottleneck in scaling AI systems keeps shifting to less-visible
  pieces of the stack — this round is chipmakers themselves betting that
  wiring, not just compute, will cap how large a training or inference
  cluster can get. For a business planning around future AI costs, it is
  one more sign that the industry sees years of infrastructure investment
  ahead of it before compute gets meaningfully cheaper, not months.
