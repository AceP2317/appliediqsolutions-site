summary: >-
  California's AI Transparency Act (SB 942, as amended by AB 853) becomes
  operative August 2, 2026 — pushed from an original January 1 start date to
  align with the EU AI Act's own provenance deadline. Any "covered provider"
  of a generative AI system with over 1 million monthly California users
  must now offer a free, public tool letting anyone check whether content
  was AI-generated, and embed a permanent, machine-readable watermark —
  provider name, system name and version, and a timestamp — in every image,
  video, and audio file it generates or alters. The California Attorney
  General and city and county attorneys can enforce it, with penalties of
  $5,000 per violation.
implications: >-
  Any business running customer-facing tools built on a large generative-AI
  platform with real California reach should check whether that platform
  counts as a "covered provider" and is actually shipping the required
  detection tool and watermarking — the obligation sits with whoever
  produces the underlying AI system, but a business relying on it for
  marketing images, video, or audio is the one that will field questions if
  disclosures are missing.
