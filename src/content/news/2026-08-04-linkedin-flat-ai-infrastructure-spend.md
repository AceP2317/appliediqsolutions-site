summary: >-
  LinkedIn says it will keep its AI data center footprint and GPU investment
  roughly flat for its next fiscal year, rather than expanding, even as
  parent company Microsoft reported $41 billion in quarterly capital
  expenditure and added 88 data centers over the past year. LinkedIn's
  engineering CTO, Erran Berger, told Wired the company roughly doubled the
  efficiency of its existing GPUs over six months through accumulated gains
  in utilization, model distillation, and workload allocation rather than
  any single breakthrough, and plans to spend that headroom on new features
  instead of new hardware.
implications: >-
  Most of the industry's AI infrastructure story in 2026 is about spending
  more; LinkedIn's is a rare public example of a company choosing to spend
  the same and get more out of it instead. For any business evaluating AI
  vendors or infrastructure, it's a reminder that raw compute spend isn't
  the only lever — how efficiently an existing setup is used matters just as
  much, and isn't something only hyperscalers can act on.
