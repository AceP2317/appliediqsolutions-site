summary: >-
  A report from AI governance vendor Opsin, based on its analysis of
  enterprise production environments across eight verticals from March 2025
  to June 2026, found companies now run roughly one AI agent per employee,
  with agent activity up 14x in the first half of 2026 alone. Of agents
  given non-default permissions, 60% were granted "allow-all" access rather
  than scoped to what their task required, and 60% ended up with
  capabilities beyond their original stated purpose. The report attributes
  much of this to the 67% of agents built by non-engineering staff in
  sales, support, and operations roles, without security review keeping
  pace.
implications: >-
  Broad, unreviewed access is exactly the kind of gap that turns a small
  automation mistake into a real breach — the same access an agent uses to
  do its job is what an attacker or a bug can misuse if nobody scoped it
  down. Any business handing an AI tool access to email, a CRM, or a
  point-of-sale system is worth a second look at whether that access
  matches the actual task, not just whatever was easiest to grant at setup.
