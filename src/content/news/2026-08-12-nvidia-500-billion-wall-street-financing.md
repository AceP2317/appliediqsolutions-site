summary: >-
  Nvidia said it is partnering with six Wall Street firms — Apollo, Blackstone,
  BlackRock, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, and KKR — to source $500 billion in
  financing for AI infrastructure, structured as debt backed by compute power
  itself as collateral. CEO Jensen Huang said he approached only those six
  firms and none turned him down, and that the money is third-party capital,
  not Nvidia's own. The deals will take the form of private offerings and
  bonds from special-purpose entities that lease compute to Nvidia's
  customers, with the first expected to reach market within months.
implications: >-
  Treating GPU compute like a bondable asset — similar to real estate or toll
  roads — is meant to make it cheaper for large AI buyers to finance data
  center buildouts, but it also ties AI infrastructure demand more tightly
  to credit markets, not just actual usage. Analysts quoted in coverage note
  it effectively subsidizes GPU costs without cutting sticker prices, and
  raises questions about how much of the boom is real demand versus
  vendor-financed deals among the same handful of companies.
