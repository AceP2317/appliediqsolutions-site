summary: >-
  A new report from AI safety nonprofit SaferAI finds that Z.ai's
  open-weight GLM-5.2 model is only a few months behind OpenAI's and
  Anthropic's frontier systems on cyber and biology capability benchmarks,
  but lacks the safety mitigations those companies apply. Testing via
  Z.ai's public API, SaferAI found GLM-5.2 refused none of the offensive
  cyber or biology tasks it was given, while Claude Opus 4.7 "refused so
  consistently that SaferAI could not complete CyberGym on it at all." Z.ai
  has not published a safety framework, pre-deployment testing
  commitments, or a risk assessment for the model, and any safeguards on
  its hosted API stop applying once the weights run independently.
implications: >-
  Capability and safety are no longer moving together: a model can match
  the frontier on raw ability while shipping with none of the guardrails
  frontier labs build in. For a business weighing a cheaper or
  open-weight AI model against a name-brand one, that's a real diligence
  question, not just a pricing one — what stops the model from doing
  something it shouldn't, and who's responsible for it once it's running
  on your own infrastructure.
