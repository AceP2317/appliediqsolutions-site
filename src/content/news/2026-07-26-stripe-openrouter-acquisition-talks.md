summary: >-
  Stripe is in talks to acquire OpenRouter, a marketplace that lets
  developers route requests across hundreds of AI models through one
  interface, in a deal that could value the startup near $10 billion, the
  Wall Street Journal reported July 23 — roughly eight times OpenRouter's
  $1.3 billion valuation from a funding round in May. OpenRouter serves more
  than five million developers by letting them compare models, switch
  providers, and avoid depending on any single AI vendor. Stripe already
  uses its own platform to process OpenRouter's transactions; other tech
  firms have reportedly evaluated bids too, and the talks could still fall
  apart.
implications: >-
  If it closes, one of the main tools businesses use to avoid depending on
  a single AI provider would itself be owned by a single company — worth
  watching for anyone using OpenRouter or a similar router specifically to
  keep model options open. It's also a fast marker of how much a
  well-positioned piece of AI infrastructure is now worth to a company
  outside the AI industry proper.
angle: >-
  A model-routing marketplace is still a rented layer between a business and
  its AI — this deal just changes who owns that layer. It's a reminder that
  routing flexibility bought from a vendor is a feature of that vendor's
  business, not a property of the business using it.
