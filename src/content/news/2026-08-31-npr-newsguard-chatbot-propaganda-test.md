title: 'AI chatbots beat search at flagging foreign disinformation, NPR test finds'
publishedAt: '2026-08-31T07:05:00-04:00'
tier: 'standard'
spotlight: false
summary: >-
  NPR and NewsGuard tested how popular chatbots like ChatGPT and Google's
  Gemini handled 30 questions built from false narratives that China, Iran,
  and Russia spread between December 2025 and July 2026 — including a false
  claim that Ukraine, not Russia, struck a historic Kyiv monastery. Chatbots
  correctly flagged the false premise about three-quarters of the time on
  average, outperforming both traditional search-engine results and the
  AI-generated summaries sitting atop Google, Bing, and DuckDuckGo results
  pages. Performance varied sharply among the summary tools: Google's AI
  Overview debunked the claims most of the time, while Microsoft's Bing
  summaries failed to push back on most of them.
implications: >-
  Anyone using an AI tool to sanity-check a claim before repeating it — in a
  customer email, a review reply, a social post — is getting meaningfully
  different reliability depending on which tool they reach for: a chatbot
  with live web search beat a plain search engine, and both beat the
  AI-summary box that now sits above most search results. The gap between
  products means "I looked it up online" and "I asked the AI" aren't
  interchangeable levels of care, and neither is which AI someone asked.
