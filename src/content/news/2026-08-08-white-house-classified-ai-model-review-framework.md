summary: >-
  The White House said it met an Aug. 1 deadline to complete a framework for
  deciding which advanced AI models must undergo a government review before
  release, but will not disclose the framework's contents, who has seen it,
  or when companies must start using it, a White House official said. The
  cybersecurity benchmarks used to assess a model, and the capability
  threshold that triggers review, are both classified. OpenAI, Anthropic and
  Google gave feedback on a draft, and the administration says it is working
  with a broader group of companies. The framework stems from a June
  executive order that created a voluntary 30-day government review window
  for frontier models before public launch.
implications: >-
  A review process billed as voluntary, built on classified benchmarks and an
  undisclosed threshold, functions as a gate that companies can't publicly
  evaluate or contest even if they want to comply in good faith. For any
  business planning around when a next-generation model will actually ship,
  that adds a government review window of unknown length and unknown
  trigger conditions to the release timeline for the largest AI labs.
