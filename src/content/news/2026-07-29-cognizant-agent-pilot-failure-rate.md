summary: >-
  Cognizant launched a dedicated EMEA AI Unit on July 28, citing IDC research
  that 88% of AI agent proofs-of-concept never reach broad production — out
  of every 33 pilots a company launches, only four go live. Gartner's 2026
  CIO survey separately found just 17% of organizations have fully deployed
  AI agents even as more than 60% expect to within two years, and projects
  over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled outright by the end of
  2027 over cost, unclear value, and weak governance. Cognizant's new unit
  targets what it says most pilots skip: governance, technology selection,
  and the data-grounding layer that keeps an agent from hallucinating a
  company's own facts.
implications: >-
  The failure mode these numbers describe isn't the AI not working — it's
  broad, ungoverned agent programs collapsing under their own scope before
  they reach anything a business actually uses day to day. For an operator
  weighing a big agentic AI rollout against something narrower and
  purpose-built, that's a real data point, not just a vendor's sales pitch.
angle: >-
  It's the same failure mode a narrowly scoped, owned tool is built to
  avoid: a system sized to one business's actual data and workflow, not a
  broad platform layered on after the fact. What Cognizant lists as commonly
  skipped — governance, the right tech choice, grounding in real data — is
  what a small, well-scoped build handles by default.
