summary: >-
  OpenAI published a full writeup, paper, and machine-checkable Lean
  formalization for a proof addressing the Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize
  problem, produced by roughly 10,000 AI agents working about 88 hours from
  September 1 to 5. The company says the result establishes two of the four
  statements in the Clay Institute's formulation but will not claim the $1
  million prize. The proof covers a "forced" version of the fluid equations;
  mathematicians Levent Alpöge and Tristan Buckmaster, using Anthropic's and
  OpenAI's own models, had published a related unforced-case result days
  earlier, and OpenAI's page now credits both after initially disputing any
  connection.
implications: >-
  It's a case study in how far a targeted swarm of coordinated AI agents can
  push a previously stalled research problem in days rather than years — and
  in how much a lab's own hedging can say about a result once the announcement
  gives way to the published substantiation. The gap between the press-call
  claim and what the company was actually willing to put in writing is the
  pattern worth watching in any AI vendor's announcement.
