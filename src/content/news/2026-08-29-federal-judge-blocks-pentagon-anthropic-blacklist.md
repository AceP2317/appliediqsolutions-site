summary: >-
  U.S. District Judge Rita Lin ruled on August 27 that the Defense
  Department's designation of Anthropic as a national-security supply
  chain risk was "illegal and baseless." Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth
  applied the label — a first for a U.S. company under this statute —
  after Anthropic refused to let Claude be used for domestic
  surveillance or autonomous weapons, cutting it off from certain
  military contracts. Lin found the move retaliated against Anthropic's
  protected stance, writing that "the empty invocation of national
  security is not a blank check to punish and retaliate against
  government critics." The government argued it stemmed from a contract
  dispute, not Anthropic's safety views.
implications: >-
  A federal court has now put a limit on the government's ability to
  punish an AI vendor for refusing a government use case on safety
  grounds, using a procurement label with no obvious ceiling on what else
  it could be applied to. For any business selling into government or
  regulated markets, it's a concrete data point on how far "safety
  policy" can survive contact with a customer's leverage.
