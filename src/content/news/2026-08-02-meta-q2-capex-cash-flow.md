title: "Meta's AI buildout is now consuming nearly all the cash it makes"
publishedAt: '2026-08-02T07:10:00-04:00'
tier: 'standard'
spotlight: false
summary: >-
  Meta's Q2 2026 revenue rose 28% year over year to $60.8 billion, beating
  expectations, but free cash flow collapsed 91% to $784 million as
  quarterly AI capital expenditure hit $31.1 billion, nearly double a year
  earlier. Meta raised its full-year capex guidance to $130–145 billion and
  issued $24.9 billion in long-term debt during the quarter rather than
  buying back stock, citing a deliberate shift toward debt to fund
  long-lived infrastructure. CEO Mark Zuckerberg said Meta is "getting a lot
  of offers for compute at a significant premium" over cost and confirmed
  plans to enter the cloud business, framing reselling compute as secondary
  to building AI services on top of it.
implications: >-
  When one of the biggest AI spenders is plowing essentially every dollar of
  operating cash into infrastructure and turning to debt to keep going, it's
  a sign that even hyperscale AI investment hasn't yet found its own
  breakeven — a useful data point for any business watching whether the AI
  compute and API pricing it depends on is likely to keep falling or
  eventually has to rise to cover this spending.
