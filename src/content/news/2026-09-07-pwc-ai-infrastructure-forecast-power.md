summary: >-
  PwC's first long-range AI infrastructure forecast, released September 2
  and built on modeling from Oxford Economics, projects $31.6 trillion in
  cumulative global data-center capital spending through 2050 across 46
  countries. Annual spending is projected to rise from roughly $800
  billion in 2026 to $1.8 trillion by 2050, with the U.S. capturing
  nearly half. The report names power availability as the single biggest
  factor determining where that investment lands, ahead of chip access,
  connectivity, security and local policy — and models a scenario where
  disrupted chip trade cuts total investment by roughly $6 trillion.
implications: >-
  This is a forecast, not a spending commitment, but the direction lines
  up with what every AI vendor is already doing: building compute
  capacity as fast as power and chips allow. For any business planning
  around AI tool costs or vendor reliability, the report's core point —
  that electricity, not chips, is becoming the binding constraint — is
  worth watching, since regional power shortages could show up as service
  slowdowns or price increases before chip shortages do.
