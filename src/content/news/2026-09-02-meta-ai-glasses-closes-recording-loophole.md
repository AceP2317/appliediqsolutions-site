title: 'Meta closes a loophole that let its AI glasses keep secretly recording'
publishedAt: '2026-09-02T07:15:00-04:00'
tier: 'standard'
spotlight: false
summary: >-
  Meta AR vice president Alex Himel announced August 31 that the company is
  updating its AI glasses so the capture LED reliably shuts off the camera
  if someone covers the light after a recording has already started —
  closing a gap in the tamper detection Meta introduced in July, which only
  caught the light being covered before recording began. Meta says
  businesses that sold services to modify or conceal the light have begun
  shutting down as a result, and it has removed posts offering those
  services along with search terms tied to covert-filming content. The
  company says only a small number of users had exploited the loophole.
implications: >-
  Wearable cameras that don't obviously look like cameras are becoming
  ordinary consumer hardware, and the norms around them — what counts as
  consent, what a business open to the public should post or announce — are
  still being written by the platform in real time, not by law. Any
  storefront or service business should assume some visitors are recording
  and plan signage or policy accordingly, rather than counting on a
  blinking light to do that work.
