summary: >-
  The Trump administration is considering a second round of semiconductor
  tariffs that would remove the data-center, startup, and consumer-device
  exemptions carved out in January's Phase 1 order, and extend duties to
  finished products built with those chips — servers, laptops, and gaming
  consoles — according to a Politico report citing eight people familiar
  with the talks. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick favors tying
  duty-free import allowances to a company's U.S. chip manufacturing
  investment, but industry says the formula can't cover the volume
  hyperscalers are procuring, and that domestic fab capacity is more
  than five years out. The measures are early-stage and would roll out
  in stages.
implications: >-
  If the exemptions that have protected AI data-center buildout since
  January come off, the cost of the servers and chips behind cloud AI
  services would rise — a cost that tends to reach customers through
  subscription and compute pricing rather than staying with the
  hyperscalers who absorb it first.
