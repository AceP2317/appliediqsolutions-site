summary: >-
  Sony Music Publishing, Warner Chappell, and other music publishers sued
  Anthropic and co-founders Dario Amodei and Benjamin Mann on August 28 in
  the Northern District of California, alleging a "brazen campaign of
  illegally torrenting, scraping, and downloading copyrighted works" —
  including song lyrics and sheet music — to train Claude. Some of the
  same lawyers already represent Universal Music Group and Concord Music
  in a separate suit filed in January. Anthropic said it disagrees with
  the claims and intends to "defend ourselves robustly in court."
implications: >-
  This builds directly on Bartz v. Anthropic, where a judge found training
  on copyrighted books legal but acquiring them through piracy was not,
  and Anthropic paid $1.5 billion to settle. The line the courts keep
  drawing — training itself can be fair use, but how the training data
  was obtained is a separate legal question — is one every business
  buying into an AI vendor's stack is exposed to secondhand.
angle: >-
  It's a reminder that a rented AI feature carries whatever legal
  uncertainty sits underneath how its underlying model was built and
  trained — exposure a business inherits without ever seeing it. Software
  built from a client's own data and their own rules doesn't carry
  someone else's unresolved copyright fight.
