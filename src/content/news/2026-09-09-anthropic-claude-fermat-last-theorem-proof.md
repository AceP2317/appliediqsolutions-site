summary: >-
  Anthropic said its Claude model worked largely autonomously over 11 days
  to write the first complete, computer-verified proof of Fermat's Last
  Theorem in the Lean formal-proof language, producing 13 million lines of
  Lean code and proving 30,300 supporting theorems along the way. The
  breakthrough followed an earlier unsuccessful attempt, and came after
  Anthropic gave Claude access to an open-source coordination tool called
  Prove2Me built by a researcher at Columbia University. Imperial College
  London mathematician Kevin Buzzard, who reviewed the proof, called it
  'this extraordinary autoformalization achievement.'
implications: >-
  This is a strong signal that AI can now handle the exhausting,
  detail-perfect work of formally verifying complex logical arguments —
  work that traditionally takes human reviewers months or years. For any
  field where accuracy depends on a person painstakingly rereading the
  logic, from contracts to financial models, it's an early look at what
  automated, machine-checked verification at scale can do.
