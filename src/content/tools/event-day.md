### One piece of arithmetic decides the day, and almost nobody does it

The pitch fee does not care how many people come. Neither does the fuel, the propane, or the extra
pair of hands you booked.

Everything you sell contributes its price minus what that one cost to make. Stack those contributions
up until they cover the costs that do not move, and that is the moment the day starts paying you.

Fixed cost divided by what one unit contributes. That is the number. It takes thirty seconds once
you have the figures in one place, and the reason it does not get done is that the figures are never
in one place — the pitch fee is in an email, the food cost is in your head, and the day is on
Saturday.

### With a menu, "a unit" means something specific

If you sell one thing, a unit is that thing and the arithmetic is clean.

Sell four things and a unit means one unit at the mix you are expecting — six tacos to every burrito,
or whatever your Saturday looks like. That assumption changes the answer materially, and it is an
assumption rather than a fact.

So it is on the page, in the open, where you can change it and watch the number move. Not buried in
a setting, and not quietly averaged behind your back. If you think the mix will be different at this
event, say so and see what it does.

### It rounds up, and that is not politeness

Half a plate does not pay half a pitch fee.

A break-even of 87.3 rounds to 88. A break-even that has not actually broken even is worse than no
number at all, because you will stand there at 87 thinking you are square when you are not.

### Prep that does not sell is costed at what it cost

Not at what it would have sold for.

That distinction matters and it is where a lot of event arithmetic goes soft. Two hundred portions
prepped, a hundred and forty sold, sixty in the bin. Those sixty cost you what the ingredients cost.
They did not cost you sixty times the menu price, because that money was never anybody's — nobody
was standing there with cash for it.

Costing waste at the selling price makes every slow day look like a disaster and every good day look
better than it was. This costs it at cost.

### The line is straight because the arithmetic is

This is the only tool here that draws its answer.

The line is straight, and it is straight because contribution per unit is a constant multiplied by a
count. Nothing is fitted to it, nothing is smoothed, and no curve has been drawn through anything.
What you are looking at is a division with a ruler through it.

### What it replaces

The decision made on gut, which is right often enough to keep being made. Or the arithmetic done on
Sunday night, after the event, when it is a fact rather than a decision.

You set your costs and your menu once. Each event is a pitch fee and a mix.

### Who it is for

Food trucks, caterers, market and festival vendors — anybody paying to be somewhere for a day and
selling on the day.

### What it will not do

**It forecasts no crowd.** It does not know how many people are coming, and neither does anybody
selling you software that says it does.

**It suggests no price.** What you charge is yours.

**It will not tell you whether to take the booking.** It tells you how many have to go out of the
window before the pitch is paid. Whether that number is reachable is a judgment about the event, the
weather, the town and your own read on it — and you have all four.

**Nothing you type is sent anywhere.** The arithmetic runs in your browser, which also means it works
in a field with one bar of signal. Close the tab and it is gone.
