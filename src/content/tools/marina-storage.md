### A rate card is not a price, it is a small system

Rate per foot, which differs by whether the boat is wet, in the stack or on the hard. A minimum
below which the bill is not worth the paper. Flat lines on top for liveaboard, for power, for
whatever this yard has decided to charge separately.

That is four things interacting, per boat, sixty or a hundred times a month. Every one of them is a
multiplication anybody can do and nobody wants to do a hundred times on the first working day of
the month.

### The minimum is the line customers query

A twenty-two foot boat on a rate per foot lands under the minimum. So it gets billed at the minimum
— which is correct, and which looks like a mistake to the person paying it, because the number does
not match their boat times the rate they were quoted.

This shows which boats landed on the minimum rather than on their footage, as a separate thing you
can see before the invoices go out. When the call comes you already know the answer, and you can
tell them in one sentence instead of going back to the card.

That is a small feature and it is the one that saves the most time, because the queried line costs
more to handle than the ten quiet ones either side of it.

### A boat under a kind that is not on the card stays unpriced

Somebody types a storage kind that does not exist on the rate card. A typo, a new arrangement, a
category the yard uses in conversation but never put on paper.

This reports the line and leaves it blank. It does not pick the nearest kind, does not fall back to
the wet rate, and does not quietly bill something plausible.

A made-up figure on an invoice is worse than a missing one. A blank gets noticed and fixed in the
office. A plausible wrong number gets sent, gets paid, and gets found in eight months by a customer
who now trusts nothing else on the bill either.

### Half-feet and whole cents

Lengths are held in half-feet and money in whole cents from the first line to the last, so a hundred
slips do not drift a fraction at a time into a total that is close to right.

On one boat this does not matter. On a hundred boats over twelve months it is the difference between
a figure you can hand to an accountant and one you have to apologize for.

### What it replaces

The spreadsheet that runs the month, built by whoever ran the office before, with the rates typed
into cells and one formula that nobody dares touch. It works. It also breaks silently the first time
somebody inserts a row, and it holds the yard's whole rate structure in a form only one person
understands.

You enter your card once — rates by kind, the minimum, the flat lines. The boat list is a paste. The
month is a read.

### Who it is for

Marinas, boatyards and dry stacks — anywhere storage is billed off a card the yard wrote, against a
list of boats that changes every month.

### What it will not do

**It suggests no rate and no minimum.** What a foot is worth at your yard depends on your water,
your depth, your town and what the yard down the creek charges — none of which a page knows, and all
of which you do.

**It does not know your slip assignments or your availability.** This prices the list you give it. It
is a billing sheet, not a yard management system, and it does not want to become one.

**It will not price a boat it cannot price.** See above. The blank is deliberate.

**Nothing you type is sent anywhere.** The arithmetic runs in your browser. Your rate card and your
customer list are two of the more commercially sensitive things a yard has, and neither leaves the
machine you are sitting at. Close the tab and it is gone.
