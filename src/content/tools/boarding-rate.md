### The same six nights price completely differently, and nothing shows it

A booking sheet shows dates. A calendar shows days. Neither one shows money.

So a six-night stay across Thanksgiving and a six-night stay the following week look identical on
every screen you own, right up until the invoice — and by then the rate has already been quoted to
somebody on the phone.

Which nights are peak is a list somebody wrote on the office wall. It is different at every kennel in
the county, it changes when the local school calendar changes, and nothing you own applies it
automatically.

### The peak rule, in dollars

This splits every booking into peak nights and standard nights, with the money each side carries.

Then it does one more thing: it works out what those same nights would have come to at the base rate
on every one of them.

The gap between those two figures is your peak rule, expressed in money. Not as a percentage, not as
a policy — as the amount of actual dollars the decision to charge more over a holiday put into the
week. That is a number most owners have never seen, and it is the one that tells you whether the
argument at the counter on the 23rd was worth having.

### Add-ons are a second business running inside the first

Baths, nail trims, medication, the extra walk. Each one is quoted per item, invoiced per item, and
totaled by nobody.

They are priced separately, delivered separately and thought about separately, which is exactly why
they never get summed. So the share of a week's take that comes from add-ons is a figure most kennels
do not have, even though it is often the part with the most room in it.

This totals them beside the board figure and shows what share they come to. That is arithmetic on
your own bookings, not a suggestion about your pricing — but it is the number that starts the
thought.

### A stay is counted from check-in to check-out

Friday to Sunday is two nights, not three.

Obvious, universally agreed, and still one of the more common quiet errors in a hand-worked
invoice — particularly on a stay that crosses a month boundary or gets extended by a day at the
desk.

The dates go in and the nights come out. There is nothing to get wrong.

### Peak nights with no peak rate entered

If you have declared a date peak but not set a rate for it, those nights fall to the base rate.

This says so out loud rather than applying the base rate silently. The silent version looks
identical to a working sheet, and you would find out in January that the whole holiday season billed
flat.

### What it replaces

The invoice worked out on the card at the desk, with the peak nights counted on fingers. Or the
booking system that holds the dates perfectly and knows nothing about your rate card.

You set your card and your peak list once. Each stay is dates, a size and whatever was added.

### Who it is for

Boarding kennels, doggy daycare, groomers and pet sitters — anywhere a stay is priced by the night
against a rate card and a calendar the owner wrote.

### What it will not do

**It suggests no nightly rate.** Yours depends on your runs, your staffing and your town.

**It names no day peak.** Which dates carry a premium is a judgment about your own demand, and a
list from anywhere else would be about somebody else's town.

**It prices no add-on.**

**It does not know whether you are full.** This prices the bookings you give it. It is not a
reservation system and does not want to become one.

**Nothing you type is sent anywhere.** The arithmetic runs in your browser. Owner names, animal names
and your rate card stay on the machine you typed them on. Close the tab and it is gone.
